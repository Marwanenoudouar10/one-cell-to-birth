# Facts used in the long film

Every claim the film makes, whether spoken or only shown, comes from the verified fact list.

- **Weeks:** all weeks are gestational (GA), counted from the first day of the last period, and "week N" means N completed weeks. Embryology sources that count from fertilisation or ovulation were converted by adding 2 weeks.
- **Used:** "spoken" means the narration states it. "Shown" means it appears in the picture only.
- **Keep it this way:** the wording rule that keeps the fact accurate. Follow it in captions, art and any rewrite.

---

## Spoken facts

### 1. Doctors count from the last period (GA 1–2)
- **Fact:** a pregnancy is dated from the first day of the last period. Fertilisation therefore falls at about week 2, and full term at week 40 is about 38 weeks after conception.
- **Used:** C02 (spoken). The counter's week 1 chip reads "last period · the count starts".
- **Keep it this way:** say it once. Ovulation shifts with cycle length, so every date is typical, not exact.
- **Sources:**
  - ACOG Committee Opinion 700: https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/2017/05000/committee_opinion_no_700__methods_for_estimating.50.aspx
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm
  - NHS, 1 to 3 weeks: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/1-to-12/1-2-3-weeks/
  - UNSW Carnegie stages ("add 2 weeks"): https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_Stages

### 2. Hundreds of millions set off; an obstacle course; only one gets in (GA 2)
- **Fact:** up to hundreds of millions of sperm set off, and only a few hundred or fewer get close to the egg. They pass gates (cervical mucus, the womb, the tube entrance), rest on the tube wall, then mature and swim on. Exactly one fertilises a normal egg.
- **Used:** C01 (spoken; the gates are shown).
- **Keep it this way:** this is an obstacle course plus a timing game, not a sprint. Put no precise count on screen.
- **Sources:**
  - Cleveland Clinic, sperm: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/sperm
  - Suarez & Pacey 2006: https://doi.org/10.1093/humupd/dmi047
  - Sakkas 2015: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4594619/
  - GLOWM (median 251 sperm in the tubes): https://www.glowm.com/section-view/heading/Sperm%20Transport%20and%20Capacitation/item/315
  - Fertile-men median count: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3812375/
  - Merck: https://www.merckmanuals.com/home/women-s-health-issues/normal-pregnancy/stages-of-fetal-development

### 3. The cell splits but doesn't grow (GA 2)
- **Fact:** the cell splits into 2, then 4, then 8, about once a day. Each new cell is smaller, so the embryo does not get bigger: it stays 0.1–0.2 mm inside a shell that cannot expand.
- **Used:** C03 (spoken). The chip stays at "0.1 mm" through the splits.
- **Keep it this way:** divisions are not perfectly in step.
- **Sources:**
  - embryology.ch: https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/preimplantation/segmentation-migration/morula.html
  - EHD stage 2: https://www.ehd.org/developmental-stages/stage2.php
  - UNSW Carnegie stages: https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_Stages

### 4. A hollow ball (GA 2)
- **Fact:** by about day 5 or 6 after fertilisation the embryo is a hollow ball (a blastocyst) of up to around a hundred cells. An inner cluster becomes the baby, and the outer shell builds the placenta.
- **Used:** C04, "Now a hollow ball" (spoken). The inner cluster is shown as a gold knot.
- **Keep it this way:** "becomes the baby" is a simplification. Cell counts vary.
- **Sources:**
  - EHD stage 3: https://www.ehd.org/developmental-stages/stage3.php
  - Hardy 1989: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2612378/
  - Cleveland Clinic, conception: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/11585-conception
  - UNSW, blastocyst: https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php?title=Blastocyst_Development

### 5. It burrows into the womb wall (GA 3)
- **Fact:** about a week after fertilisation the embryo starts to burrow into the womb wall, and a few days later it is completely buried.
- **Used:** C04 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:** leave out loss statistics. Light spotting happens to some people, not all.
- **Sources:**
  - Wilcox 1999 (NEJM): https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199906103402304
  - Wilcox 1999 (PubMed): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10362823/
  - Embryo Project (ASU): https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/process-implantation-embryos-primates
  - Merck: https://www.merckmanuals.com/home/women-s-health-issues/normal-pregnancy/stages-of-fetal-development
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm

### 6. The hormone a pregnancy test detects (GA 3–4)
- **Fact:** as it attaches, the embryo's outer cells (the future placenta) start making hCG, the hormone a pregnancy test detects. A home test is most reliable around the missed period (about GA 4w0d).
- **Used:** C04, "sends a message" (spoken). The test strip's line is shown at W04.
- **Keep it this way:** don't call it the embryo's "first" message, and don't promise a test works on a given day.
- **Sources:**
  - MedlinePlus, pregnancy test: https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/pregnancy-test/
  - Cleveland Clinic, pregnancy tests: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/9703-pregnancy-tests
  - Cole 2010: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2936313/
  - Nwabuobi 2017: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5666719/
  - NHS, 4 weeks: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/1-to-12/4-weeks/

### 7. Three layers that build the body (GA 4)
- **Fact:** in week 4 the embryo is a tiny flat disc that sorts its cells into three layers, and these build every part of the body:
  - the outer layer: brain and nerves, and skin
  - the middle layer: heart and vessels, bone and muscle
  - the inner layer: gut and lungs
- **Used:** C05 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:** the lists are simplified. The dates are approximate.
- **Sources:**
  - UNSW, gastrulation: https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Gastrulation
  - UNSW Carnegie stages: https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_Stages
  - Tyser 2021 (Nature): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04158-y

### 8. First heartbeats (GA 5) and a beating heart by week 6
- **Fact:** around week 5 (5w0d–5w2d) a tiny tube of heart cells starts squeezing by itself, in an embryo about 2 mm long. Week 6 is when a vaginal scan usually first picks it up.
- **Used:** C06 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:**
  - It is a tube, not a four-chambered heart.
  - "By week six, a tiny heart is beating" is the safe wording.
  - The teaser's "week six: starts to beat" was a week late.
  - The first beat has never been watched directly in a human, so its exact onset is debated by a few days.
- **Sources:**
  - Männer 2022: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9225347/
  - NHS, week 5: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-5/
  - Cleveland Clinic, fetal development: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm

### 9. First movements (GA 7–8)
- **Fact:** at about 7 to 7½ weeks the embryo makes its very first movements, tiny sideways bends of about a second, months before anyone can feel them.
- **Used:** C07 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:** the movements are spontaneous, made by spinal and brainstem circuits. Never say "waves" or "chooses".
- **Sources:**
  - Lüchinger 2008 (PubMed): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18091359/
  - Lüchinger 2008 (journal): https://www.nature.com/articles/pr200837
  - Einspieler 2021: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33973235/
  - NHS, your baby's movements: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/

### 10. Webbed fingers and a little tail, both fade (GA 8–10)
- **Fact:** the fingers start out joined by webbing, and between weeks 8 and 10 the webbing cells die off on cue, freeing the fingers. A similar wave of planned cell death, helped by the body outgrowing it, removes the embryo's tiny tail by about week 10.
- **Used:** C08 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:**
  - The tail is an extension of the developing spine, nerve cord and somites. It is not a monkey tail: it never wags, and must not be linked to the rare "human tail" anomaly.
  - Keep the tone "sculpting", never violent.
- **Sources:**
  - Montero 2020: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7644521/
  - Embryo Project (ASU), apoptosis: https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/apoptosis-embryonic-development
  - Carnegie stages monograph: https://publicationsonline.carnegiescience.edu/publications_online/developmental_stages.pdf
  - Cleveland Clinic, fetal development: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth
  - Fallon & Simandl 1978: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/677043/
  - Tojima 2018: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29315541/

### 11. The embryo becomes a fetus (GA 10)
- **Fact:** as the counter reaches week 10 (8 weeks after fertilisation), with every major organ in place, the embryo is called a fetus. It is about 3 cm long.
- **Used:** C09 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:** "in place" does not mean mature. The dividing line is a convention, not a sudden event.
- **Sources:**
  - ACOG, how your fetus grows: https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/how-your-fetus-grows-during-pregnancy
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm
  - Carnegie stages monograph: https://publicationsonline.carnegiescience.edu/publications_online/developmental_stages.pdf
  - O'Rahilly & Müller 2010: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20185898/

### 12. Fingers, toes, a face (GA 12, the teaser beat)
- **Fact:** the face takes shape in weeks 7–8, and the fingers and toes are separate by about weeks 10–11. By week 12 there are separate fingers and toes and a formed face, with the eyelids closed.
- **Used:** C09 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:** sources differ by about a week on when the fingers are free. Toes lag slightly behind fingers.
- **Sources:**
  - Carnegie stages monograph: https://publicationsonline.carnegiescience.edu/publications_online/developmental_stages.pdf
  - Cleveland Clinic, week 10 ("no more webbed fingers"): https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth
  - NHS, week 9: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-9/
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm

### 13. Thumb-sucking (GA 15)
- **Fact:** by about week 15 the baby can suck its thumb.
- **Used:** C10 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:** the right-thumb link to handedness comes from one group's follow-up of 75 children, so it is left out. Show no handedness cue.
- **Sources:**
  - Cleveland Clinic, fetal development: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth
  - NHS, 13 weeks: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/13-to-27/13-weeks/
  - Hepper 1991: https://doi.org/10.1016/0028-3932(91)90080-r

### 14. Fingerprints set for life, different even in identical twins (GA 17)
- **Fact:** the fingertip ridges are set for life by about week 17. They are unique, even different from an identical twin's, and form as waves spreading from a few start points.
- **Used:** C10 (spoken). The ripple waves are shown.
- **Keep it this way:** say "different", not "nothing alike": twins' overall pattern types are often similar.
- **Sources:**
  - NIJ Fingerprint Sourcebook, chapter 3: https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225323.pdf
  - Glover 2023 (Cell): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2023.01.015
  - News-Medical summary of Glover: https://www.news-medical.net/news/20230315/How-do-fingerprints-develop.aspx
  - NHS, 17 weeks: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/13-to-27/17-weeks/

### 15. The first movement its mother feels (GA 16–24, often about 20)
- **Fact:** the baby has been moving for months, but now it is big and strong enough for its mother to feel it for the first time: a flutter at first, later real kicks.
- **Used:** C11 (spoken). The womb wall that stayed still in C07 now ripples.
- **Keep it this way:**
  - It is the first kick FELT, not the first movement.
  - With a first baby it may come after 20 weeks.
  - Give no kick counts; reduced movement is a reason to call a midwife.
- **Sources:**
  - NHS, your baby's movements: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/
  - NHS, 18 weeks: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/13-to-27/18-weeks/
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm
  - de Vries 1985: https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-3782(85)90174-4

### 16. It can hear its mother's heartbeat and tummy (GA 19–22)
- **Fact:** around week 19 scans first show the baby reacting to sound, low and middle pitches first. Soon it can hear its mother's heartbeat, breathing and stomach rumbles; Cleveland Clinic places this at week 22.
- **Used:** C12 (spoken). The week-19 reaction is shown in C11.
- **Keep it this way:** never say "understands" or "recognises" in the second trimester.
- **Sources:**
  - Hepper & Shahidullah 1994: https://doi.org/10.1136/fn.71.2.f81
  - Cleveland Clinic, fetal development: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth
  - NHS, 21 weeks: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/13-to-27/21-weeks/

### 17. The eyelids open and it blinks (GA 26–28)
- **Fact:** the eyelids, sealed shut since about weeks 10–11, open for the first time around weeks 26–28, and the baby begins to blink.
- **Used:** C12 (spoken, as "around week twenty-seven"). The seal is planted in C09.
- **Keep it this way:** the womb is dim, not dark. The baby cannot "see" images. Keep the iris dark and neutral.
- **Sources:**
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm
  - NHS, 26 weeks: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/13-to-27/26-weeks/
  - Cleveland Clinic, week 27: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth
  - Birnholz & Benacerraf 1983: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6623091/
  - Byun 2011: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21416630/

### 18. Brain growth spurt, about four teaspoons a week (GA 29–40)
- **Fact:** the brain adds about 22 mL of tissue a week (about 4.5 teaspoons), while its surface crinkles into more and more folds.
- **Used:** C13 (spoken, as "about four teaspoons").
- **Keep it this way:**
  - The figure is an average, measured in premature babies.
  - Never say "faster than ever": growth is similar just after birth.
  - The brain is already partly folded at 29 weeks.
- **Sources:**
  - Hüppi 1998: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9485064/
  - Yehuda 2026: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13022036/
  - Ball 2024 (preprint): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10888819/
  - Holland 2014: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111045/
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm

### 19. Practice breathing, with no air (GA 30–40)
- **Fact:** long before its first breath of air, the baby practises breathing, moving its chest and diaphragm in rhythm for up to about 30% of the time. The lungs hold fluid, and the oxygen comes from the placenta.
- **Used:** C14 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:** show no air and no bubbles. Never say "breathes amniotic fluid". The movements come in episodes, with normal pauses.
- **Sources:**
  - Sanchez-Esteban 2013: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3872295/
  - Patrick 1980: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7383483/
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm

### 20. Lungs probably ready by week 36 (GA 34–36)
- **Fact:** surfactant coats the air sacs. Most babies make enough to breathe normally by week 34, and by week 36 the lungs are probably mature enough to breathe without help.
- **Used:** C14 (spoken, as "probably ready"). The surfactant film is shown.
- **Keep it this way:**
  - Keep "probably".
  - The lungs are not finished: most air sacs form after birth.
  - Sources disagree on when surfactant starts (weeks 24–30), so name no start week.
- **Sources:**
  - NHS, neonatal respiratory distress syndrome: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/neonatal-respiratory-distress-syndrome/
  - NHS, week 36: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-36/
  - Stanford Children's: https://www.stanfordchildrens.org/en/topic/default?id=respiratory-distress-syndrome-rds-90-P02371
  - MedlinePlus, respiratory distress syndrome: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001563.htm
  - Schittny 2017: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5320013/

### 21. It knows its mother's voice from a stranger's (GA 31–40)
- **Fact:** by the end of pregnancy the baby can tell its mother's voice from a stranger's, and after birth it prefers hers.
- **Used:** C15 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:**
  - This is telling a voice apart, not understanding words.
  - Never say "her voice speeds its heart": that rise came from a recording, and live speech gave mixed results.
  - No claims about recognising the father or music.
- **Sources:**
  - Kisilevsky 2003: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12741744/
  - DeCasper & Fifer 1980: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7375928/
  - Voegtline 2013: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3858412/
  - NHS, week 31: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-31/

### 22. Full term (GA 39–40)
- **Fact:** at 39–40 weeks the baby is "full term" (39+0 to 40+6), at about 3.3 kg and 50 cm.
- **Used:** C16 (spoken). The chip reads 49.9 cm.
- **Keep it this way:** NHS calls week 37 full term, but ACOG's definition is used here. Don't add the due-date odds.
- **Sources:**
  - ACOG Committee Opinion 579: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24150030/
  - NICHD: https://www.nichd.nih.gov/newsroom/resources/spotlight/102413-redefining-term
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm
  - NHS, week 40: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-40/

### 23. A first breath of air (birth)
- **Fact:** with its first breaths the baby's lungs fill with air for the first time. Within minutes its heart reroutes the blood, closing a small doorway (the foramen ovale) it used in the womb.
- **Used:** C16. The lungs filling is spoken; the rerouting is shown only as one soft glow.
- **Keep it this way:**
  - Breathing in clears most of the fluid.
  - The ductus closes over 1–3 days, not instantly.
  - Newborns may not cry straight away.
- **Sources:**
  - Hillman 2012: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3504352/
  - MSD Manual, perinatal physiology: https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/perinatal-physiology/perinatal-physiology
  - Embryo Project (ASU), circulatory changes at birth: https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/circulatory-changes-birth
  - Stanford Children's, fetal circulation: https://www.stanfordchildrens.org/en/topic/default?id=fetal-circulation-90-P01790

### 24. The grip (newborn)
- **Fact:** press a finger into a newborn's palm and it grips tight. If you try to pull away, the grip tightens.
- **Used:** C16 (spoken).
- **Keep it this way:**
  - It is an automatic reflex that needs pressure on the palm, and it fades by about 4–6 months.
  - Never suggest testing it, and never show a baby hanging by its grip.
- **Sources:**
  - MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003292.htm
  - Futagi 2012: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3384944/
  - Cleveland Clinic, newborn reflexes: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/23265-newborn-reflexes

### 25. One cell has become a whole new human (callback)
- **Fact:** the whole body comes from one fertilised cell (the zygote), which holds 46 chromosomes, 23 from each parent.
- **Used:** C16 (spoken, the teaser line). The gold speck echoes C01–C02.
- **Sources:**
  - Mayo Clinic: https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/pregnancy-week-by-week/in-depth/prenatal-care/art-20045302
  - EHD stage 1: https://www.ehd.org/developmental-stages/stage1.php

## Week counter data

- **Weeks 4–40:** `sizes.json` is the verified weekly table, copied verbatim. Its comparisons are pregnancy-app style (lime at week 12, pumpkin at week 40). Length is crown-rump to week 19, then crown-heel from week 20, which is why the chip jumps from 15.3 to 25.6 cm.
  - **Week 4:** the table gives 1 mm, a mid-week value (Carnegie stage 8 is 1.0–1.5 mm). NHS and Cleveland Clinic give about 2 mm by the END of week 4. The narration says only "week four", so nothing conflicts.
  - **Weights:** the table's weights are app-style estimates and differ from the WHO estimated-fetal-weight medians in the fact list (for example 875 g against 1039 g at week 27). The narration never quotes a weight. If the chip shows weight, it shows the table's value (3.5 kg at week 40, which is within the normal range).
- **Weeks 1–3:** the stage chip (see storyboard.md) uses 0.1 mm for the egg, the zygote and the cleavage stages, and 0.1–0.2 mm for the blastocyst and implantation.
  - EHD stages 1–3: https://www.ehd.org/developmental-stages/stage1.php
  - UNSW Carnegie stages 3–5: https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_Stages
- **Week 1 wording:** the chip reads "last period · the count starts", not "no baby yet". Shown over a fertilised cell, "no baby yet" reads as a political statement.
